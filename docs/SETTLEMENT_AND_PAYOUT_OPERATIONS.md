# Settlement and Payout Operations

Bank transfer to the organizer’s approved account is the standard. Send the available balance within 2–3 business days after the event once reconciliation and approval are complete. Hold about 10% of eligible proceeds for chargebacks and release the remaining reserve 30 days after the event, subject to open refunds, disputes and documented holds. Record check payouts only for approved exceptions.

## Statement lifecycle

Admin → Payouts → **Due** shows which events and reserves need finance and by when; see [Bank-transfer payouts](./BANK_TRANSFER_PAYOUTS.md#admin--payouts--due).

1. Complete or cancel the event. The hourly `CompletePastEventsJob` completes published events 6 hours after their end time (system actor, audited as `event.completed` with source `system`); an organizer manager can complete earlier. Events without an end time are not completed automatically.
2. Resolve every pending refund and open dispute.
3. Review the live preview: gross, discounts, buyer refunds, net charged, buyer-paid HafaPass fees, actual processing costs deducted from the organizer, processing covered by HåfaPass, organizer proceeds, reserves, adjustments, payable amount, prior payouts, and negative balance. Each order's processing-fee bearer snapshot decides which processing column it uses; see [Stripe processing fee evidence](./STRIPE_PROCESSING_FEE_OPERATIONS.md#who-bears-the-processing-cost).
4. Finalize the statement. An identical ledger returns the existing version; changed activity creates the next immutable version.
5. Confirm the connected account has current provider capabilities, no provider requirements, and an unexpired independent readiness approval; then confirm the organization-wide available balance is sufficient.
6. Submit the payout with a unique idempotency key.
7. Reconcile processing payouts to paid, failed, or reversed from provider/bank evidence. Every transition is audited.

Archive an event only after its money is closed. `EventLifecycle` refuses `archive` for anything but a draft while any finalization precondition is open (`Settlements::Finalizer.open_money_blocker`: unresolved cancellation refunds, pending refunds, open disputes, open reconciliation exceptions, unverified Stripe processing fees), and unless the latest finalized statement's digest still matches the live calculation (a payout counts as activity, so finalize again after the final payout), no payout for the event is pending, processing or held, nothing remains payable (`payable_cents - paid_cents`), and no reserve is held. Archived events cannot be finalized. If an event was archived before this guard existed, or too early, an administrator can use **Unarchive** in Admin → Events (`POST /api/v1/admin/events/:id/unarchive`, reason required, audited as `event.unarchived`); it returns the event to the status it was archived from.

The organizer's **Request payout** action reserves a request. A `processing` result does not confirm a transfer. Production simulation cannot mark a payout paid. Manual processing remains reserved until an administrator records a confirmed outcome.

## Recording a confirmed outcome

The administrator endpoint `PATCH /api/v1/admin/payouts/:id` accepts `status` (`paid`, `failed`, or `reversed`), an optional `provider_payout_id`, and an `evidence` object containing:

- `reference`: an opaque reference to the restricted bank/provider confirmation;
- `confirmed_at`: the actual confirmation timestamp in ISO8601 with an offset, after the request and not in the future;
- `reason`: what was reconciled, including the matched cents and outcome.

Recording `paid` also freezes the payout's **custody determination** (`payout_custody_determinations`, A2 R2-2), in the same transaction. It records whether the payout's finalized settlement version funded money HåfaPass held as Stripe custody (`stripe_custody`, `non_custodial` or `ambiguous`), with the funded lines and qualifying payments. The BPT detail CSV reads it, so a later Stripe capture, classification, backfill or audit redaction never changes which payouts a month reports. The row is append-only, and a reversal or check adjustment never rewrites it. It does not change who may record a payout or when. Payouts recorded paid before A2 get theirs once from `bin/rails payout_custody:derive` (dry run by default; `DRY_RUN=0` writes, as part of the authorized A2 release). Until then, and for an `ambiguous` result, BPT uses the conservative rule described in the [CPA guide](./CPA_MONTHLY_EXPORT_GUIDE.md) and lists the payout in `payout_custody_alerts`.

Recording `paid` requires a real provider/bank transfer identifier; `sim_*` and `manual_po_*` placeholders are rejected. A reversal retains the original transfer identifier and records its own confirmation reference. Failed/reversed outcomes also require confirmation evidence before releasing reserved funds. Store bank documents in the restricted evidence location and send only their reference, never account credentials or full bank documents.

The status change and append-only actor/evidence audit commit together under the organization's payout lock. Competing terminal outcomes cannot both win. An exact retry returns the saved outcome; changed evidence or a conflicting outcome is refused. This endpoint records an operator's confirmation; it does not query a bank, send money, or independently verify the supplied document.

Late submission results use the same locks. A late processing reply or error cannot overwrite an evidence-backed terminal outcome. A conflicting paid reply reopens `processing` with `provider_result_conflict`, preserves the earlier transfer identity and both outcomes in the audit trail, and blocks organization payouts. Review the conflict and restricted confirmation evidence before resolving it. The refusal response supplies `conflict_audit_id`; the new reconciliation's `evidence.conflict_audit_id` must acknowledge that exact latest conflict. Retrying the earlier request without this acknowledgment keeps the hold.

Automated production payout adapters remain disabled. Before enabling one, implement and test crash-safe dispatch ownership that prevents a failed/reversed reconciliation from releasing funds while its original provider submission can still pay. Session advisory locks require a direct PostgreSQL connection and must not be added to the pooled application URL. The current manual gateway makes no external transfer; administrator evidence records the separate bank outcome.

## Adjustment rules

- Use `reserve_hold` with a negative amount and `reserve_release` with a positive amount.
- Use `manual_credit` and `manual_debit` only with a specific reconciliation reason and supporting evidence.
- Reverse an adjustment through its reversal operation; do not delete it.
- Never attach an event, order, or dispute from another organization.
- A lost dispute is sourced directly from the dispute ledger. Do not duplicate it with a manual `dispute_loss` adjustment.
- From A2, never link a `manual_credit` or `manual_debit` to a classified never-fulfilled order (`order_id`) or to a dispute on a classified payment (`dispute_id`). Such a cash adjustment is not the organizer's: the calculator shows it at $0 ("not counted"), the payout balance, organization summary and CPA export (custody and bridge) leave it out, and it is listed as `classified_linked_adjustment` in the CPA and BPT alerts. Its reversal is treated the same way. It also stops an over-return on that payment from counting as platform-only, so the event's payouts stay held until finance resolves the `never_fulfilled_buyer_overpaid` item. A `reserve_hold` or `reserve_release` is an organizer control and is booked as usual even when linked. An adjustment linked to the fulfilled order of a second payment (payment A's order, with no dispute on payment B) is an ordinary organizer adjustment.
- A never-fulfilled capture (a card payment that succeeded after HåfaPass released its order, refunded automatically, no tickets issued; `Commerce::NeverFulfilledCapture`) is never charged to the organizer: its Stripe processing is a HåfaPass cost and a lost dispute on it is not a chargeback. The CPA export books both against the buyer refund columns instead. Who bears that processing needs Leon/CPA confirmation; the switch is `Commerce::NeverFulfilledCapture::ORGANIZER_BEARS_PROCESSING`, snapshotted onto each classification, so a later change is prospective. From A2 the statement no longer shows that processing as "covered by HåfaPass" (see the A2 rules below). Deploying this booking rule changes the source digest of every event with a never-fulfilled capture that has Stripe processing or a lost dispute (the dispute line no longer appears), and flipping the switch changes it again for events with such processing. After either change, finalize those events' statements again before paying; `PayoutCreator` refuses a stale digest. See [Never-fulfilled captures design](./design/never-fulfilled-captures.md) for the planned replacement.

## Incident rules

- If provider submission is uncertain, retry with the same idempotency key.
- If any sale, refund, dispute, fee, reserve, adjustment, or payout changed after finalization, finalize a new version before paying.
- A failed payout releases its reservation; a reversed payout restores funds only after the reversal is recorded. On an event with a never-fulfilled capture, a reversal is recorded only with the bank's exact return evidence (`return_reference`, `returned_amount_cents` equal to the amount that left, `returned_at` after the transfer left and not in the future; API-only), so a transfer that left is never released on the generic confirmation alone. The database refuses any other reversal there at commit, from any release or raw SQL. A transfer that never left is recorded `failed`. Other events keep their existing reversal rule; return evidence given there is verified the same way.
- A post-payout liability produces a negative balance and reduces later organization payouts. Do not zero it manually.
- A cancellation hold cannot be released while any buyer refund remains in the reconciliation queue, even with prior-send confirmation. If the check was physically sent before cancellation, record the held payout as `paid` with the admin-only confirmation, reason, and bank evidence. The queue marks the remaining buyer liability for organizer recovery. Organizer finances subtract that unresolved liability immediately; the organization-wide payout balance carries the debit into future payout eligibility. Once refunds succeed, the refund ledger replaces the provisional debit.
- The daily refund reconciliation sweep processes oldest pending refunds first. `REFUND_RECONCILIATION_MAX_REFUNDS` (default 200), `REFUND_RECONCILIATION_MAX_STRIPE_CALLS` (default 500), and `REFUND_RECONCILIATION_MAX_CONSECUTIVE_ERRORS` (default 5) bound each run. A missing provider refund ID is searched for at most three Stripe pages. Ambiguous or incomplete matches stay open for manual finance review.
- One open reconciliation exception linked to any order (directly or through its payment) holds every payout for that organization and blocks finalizing that event's statement. Review them in Admin → Reconciliation (`/admin/reconciliation_exceptions`): the **Payouts on hold** panel lists each held organization and blocked event. Late captures offer **Refund buyer** (`Commerce::LateCaptureRefund`, the same repayment workflow as the automatic job); early fraud warnings link to the order's fraud refund; external Stripe refunds link to Refund issues. Resolving needs a note and closes the record only; it never moves money, so the server refuses it where that could strand money. A late capture that still owes the buyer (or has a refund pending) cannot be resolved with a note: use **Refund buyer**, or **Retry refund** in Refund issues if the automatic refund failed; the refund resolves it. External Stripe refund codes and `card_present_result_unknown` need `verified_no_ledger_change: true` plus an evidence note of at least 20 characters (audited as `reconciliation_exception.resolved_without_ledger_change`); an early fraud warning resolved without the fraud refund needs `reviewed_no_action: true` and an evidence note (audited as `reconciliation_exception.reviewed_no_action`). Other codes resolve with a note (audited as `reconciliation_exception.manually_resolved`). **Refund buyer** writes `reconciliation_exception.buyer_refund_requested` after the Stripe call, with the refund ID and outcome (`succeeded`, `requested`, `unknown` or `failed`). Admin → Maintenance → complete past events runs `CompletePastEventsJob` immediately with the administrator as actor (same 6-hour grace period).
- From A1-R1, a payment HåfaPass owes back in full (a never-fulfilled capture, or one with a late-capture, second-capture, ambiguous-ownership or classification-quarantine exception) is returned only by the repayment workflow (`Commerce::BuyerRepayment`): one operation at a time, each with its own idempotency key, sized to what is still owed after other refunds and lost disputes. In A1-R1 that workflow sends nothing; **Refund buyer** and retries explain the wait ("starts with the next release"), and the exception stays open. A database fence refuses an older release's refund or payout change on these payments and events. No payout on an event with an open never-fulfilled item is created, released, submitted, finalized or recorded as paid. The one exception is a transfer whose submission was authorized before the item opened and whose money actually left before it: record it as paid in this release with `sent_before_hold_confirmed: true` and the bank's transfer time, which must be earlier than the hold. The database checks all three for every release (the submission audit, the transfer time and this release's confirmation), so an older release cannot record paid on a held event at all, and a submission or a timestamp alone never proves a transfer. A payout still in flight from before the hold can be recorded as `processing`. A capture an older release acknowledged without classifying it (a second payment on a sold order, or a further payment on an order already marked never fulfilled) holds the event the same way until this release classifies or quarantines it. The hold starts when that capture was recorded, and classifying or quarantining it does not change that time, so a transfer that left after the capture still cannot be recorded as paid. Payout submission and paid recording wait for a capture still being recorded on the same event. If an older release is running during a deploy or after a rollback, it refuses these steps ("has a sale or capture in progress"); retry once the capture is recorded. These holds close only when their evidence is recorded; a note cannot close a second-capture, ambiguous-ownership, quarantine, backfill, reopened or sale-effects hold. A refund or lost dispute on a payment that is not proven to own the sale (for example a second payment before it is classified) is recorded without touching the sale, under `never_fulfilled_sale_effects_held`, until classification or ownership evidence decides it. A never-fulfilled order is not a sale: its proceeds and the refunds and losses on them stay out of the statement and the payout balance. The one item that can be non-blocking is `never_fulfilled_buyer_overpaid`: the buyer received more than they paid through outside refunds or disputes. From A2 it alerts finance without holding the organizer only while the capture is **proven platform-only** (database function `hafapass_overpaid_platform_only`): every refund and lost dispute on it is posted and recognized, nothing is pending, open or unknown, no evidence hold is open on it, no balance adjustment is linked to it, and no payout custody determination names it. Otherwise it holds the event's payouts and finalization like every other item, in the readers and in the database fences, until finance resolves it with a note (P4).

**A2 payout entry rules.** On an event with a never-fulfilled capture, a payout is approved against one exact statement version. Releasing it from hold, submitting it and recording it paid each check that this version is still the current statement. A version finalized before A2, or before a never-fulfilled capture it does not show, is out of date. On an out-of-date version:
- **Release and submission are refused** ("approved statement is out of date"). Finalize the statement again and pay from the new version. The new version subtracts every payout already reserved or sent, so it only offers what is still owed.
- **Paid can be recorded only for a transfer that already left.** That means a transfer submitted before the statement changed, with the bank's transfer time before then. Record it with `sent_before_restatement_confirmed: true` (the hold's `sent_before_hold_confirmed` covers it when the hold started no later), the transfer reference and its time. "When the statement changed" is the earliest proven change (`PayoutEntryGuard.paid_cutoff`): the restatement for a never-fulfilled capture the version does not show, or, for an ordinary change such as a refund, dispute, adjustment or new sale line after approval, that line's own durable recording time (for a refund, its database-stamped success time). A transfer time at or after that moment is refused. The database checks the restatement case for every release; the ordinary-change case is checked by this release, the only one that can record paid. The audit keeps the version, the cutoff and each changed line with its time.
- **When the change cannot be dated, finance decides.** If a changed line has no recorded time after the approval (for example an existing processing line whose amount was corrected), the confirmation alone is refused. Record it only with a finance disposition: `stale_disposition_reason` (20–1000 characters: why this transfer is the organizer's) and `stale_disposition_reference` (the finance ticket). The disposition is its own audit row (`payout.stale_statement_disposition`) with the changed lines. A disposition is refused when the change is dated. Both fields are API-only; an authorized admin sends them on `PATCH /api/v1/admin/payouts/:id`.
- **Before sending a manual transfer or writing a check, check Admin → Payouts.** The manual gateway records only a placeholder (`manual_po_<id>`, shown as "Bank transfer not recorded yet"); the money leaves when finance sends it. The list re-checks every payout entry guard when it loads, the same list submission and paid recording read (`PayoutEntryGuard.blockers`), on every event. "Approval is current" means none applies. A red "Do not send" note names each one that does: the statement changed, a buyer repayment is unresolved, card processing on a returned payment is waiting for review, buyer refunds on the event are unresolved, or the payee's bank details changed since approval. Do not send. If it already left, record it as above: for the repayment hold or the processing review, with `sent_before_hold_confirmed: true` and a transfer time and submission before the guard began; for a statement change, as described above; for unresolved refunds and changed bank details, with their existing confirmations. Otherwise record it failed and reissue it. The note clears when the guard is resolved. Reload the list right before sending.
- **A reservation that was never sent** is marked failed with a reason ("Reissued on the current statement"), then reissued on the new version. Nothing is deleted. Earlier versions, paid transfers and their custody rows stay as they are.
- **A transfer the provider accepted after the payout's hold or statement change** (a provider adapter that reports paid while a fence refuses it) is kept as a provider conflict: in flight, with the provider's reference, never sent again. It is never recorded as an ordinary paid payout. Finance confirms it with `status: "accepted_transfer"` on `PATCH /api/v1/admin/payouts/:id`, with the usual reference, reason and `confirmed_at`, and the `conflict_audit_id` of the conflict audit. That writes `payout.accepted_transfer_recorded`; the payout stays in flight and its amount stays committed, so neither the same nor a fresh idempotency key can pay it again. The same applies when the provider reports paid after an ordinary statement change or while the organizer-borne processing review is open. Its money left, so it is never recorded failed, and a later provider result does not change it. If the bank returns it, first record the accepted transfer (if the return arrives before that, the reversal is refused until the acceptance is recorded), then record `status: "reversed"` with the same transfer identifier, the conflict acknowledgement and the return evidence: `return_reference`, `returned_amount_cents` (the full amount that left) and `returned_at` (after the acceptance, not in the future). Only then is its reservation released. The CPA workpaper counts the accepted transfer once in `organizer_payouts_made` (the month finance recorded it) and the verified return as a reversal. The admin list shows it as `provider_accepted` with only "reversed" allowed; the return evidence is API-only. The manual gateway never reports paid, so this applies only to a future provider adapter.
- **A2 payouts are never inserted already paid.** A payout row inserted as paid commits only with its custody determination (the authorized historical derivation). Payouts become paid only by being recorded paid.

During the A2 deploy and after a rollback, an older release cannot act on these events at all: it cannot finalize, create, release, submit or record an outcome. On every event it also cannot record a payout as paid, because paid now writes the payout's custody determination in the same transaction and only A2 does that. Record those steps again once A2 is serving. `bin/rails never_fulfilled:a2_preflight` (read-only) lists each affected event's payable, reservations and sent transfers, and the action left for each in-flight payout.

**A2 statement and balance rules.** Each classified capture shows on the statement as one $0.00 line, **"Payment received, order unfulfilled"** (one per never-fulfilled order, and one per second payment on a fulfilled order). Its note gives the live return status ("Owed to the buyer", "Being returned to the buyer", "Partly returned; the rest is owed to the buyer", "Return to the buyer being confirmed" or "Returned to the buyer"), read from the buyer ledger when the page loads. The status is not stored and is not part of the approval digest, so later returns never force a new approval. The line's stored metadata (order, payments, captured amount) is fixed at classification. Card processing on a classified capture follows the bearer snapshotted at classification: under `platform` (the default) it is a HåfaPass cost and appears in no organizer figure, including "covered by HåfaPass"; under `organizer` it is its own statement item ("Card processing on an unfulfilled payment") and changes the digest. On a second payment (payment scope), an organizer-borne fee has no fee component yet: its review holds finalization and every payout on the event until finance books the exact cost. A never_fulfilled_buyer_overpaid item stops holding the organizer only when the capture is proven platform-only, which includes positive funding proof: every payout on the event that moved or may move money was approved on a version that shows the capture's $0 line. Its hold, once the proof fails, starts at the first fact that defeated it after the event's last passing fence and never moves later while it fails. On the organizer's statement, a manual transfer finance approved but has not sent shows as "Approved, not sent yet", and the summary says HåfaPass is preparing it; "on its way" means the money has left (a real provider or bank reference). A pending refund or open dispute on a classified payment is never an organizer debit. While one is unresolved the organization's available payout balance is held at $0.00, and cancellation recovery leaves it out. Organization finance totals, sales analytics, the orders CSV and event stats leave classified orders out and never count a second payment's refunds against the fulfilled sale. A2 changes the digest of every event with a classified capture, so finalize those events again before paying (design §8.8). Event cancellation refunds leave these payments to the repayment workflow. See [the A1 release runbook](./NEVER_FULFILLED_RELEASE.md).
- Escalate any cent variance, unexplained provider object, cross-currency activity, or payout without evidence; do not force reconciliation.

## Release evidence

For each production provider, retain the onboarding evidence, provider approval, connected-account identifier, signed terms, test transaction identifiers, settlement version and digest, payout identifier, bank receipt, reconciliation result, approving finance user, and audit-log request IDs. Link the restricted bundle through the append-only payment-readiness submission and independent approval described in [Gate B Payment Readiness Operations](./GATE_B_PAYMENT_READINESS_OPERATIONS.md).

Before a paid public pilot, complete the real chronological charge → partial refund → settlement → payout/bank receipt → final refund → negative-balance settlement proof and approval in [Gate H Low-Value Live-Money Proof](./GATE_H_LIVE_MONEY_PROOF.md). A simulated payout, a `processing` manual transfer, or a provider dashboard screenshot without matching local/bank cents is not Gate H evidence.
